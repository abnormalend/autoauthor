Paranormal romance is a romance first. The supernatural is not the subject
of the book; it is the pressure applied to a couple, and everything this
pack scores asks how hard that pressure presses on these two specifically.

That makes the genre easy to misjudge in one particular direction, so this
pack says it plainly at the top. A paranormal romance is not an
under-built fantasy novel. It is a book that has correctly declined to
build one. Its world is deliberately thin past the reach of the couple —
no dynastic history, no mapped continent, no iceberg of implied depth
beneath the waterline — because attention spent there is attention taken
off the relationship, which is the plot. Judging a paranormal romance
against epic fantasy's worldbuilding standards penalizes the book for
being correctly in its genre. This pack therefore declares
`conflicts_with: ["fantasy"]`: loading fantasy alongside it would import
history, geography, and iceberg-depth dimensions that a good paranormal
romance is right not to satisfy. A book that genuinely wants an
independent world plot and a full magic system is a different shelf and
wants a different primary.

The pack this one is designed to sit beside is `romance`, as the
secondary. This pack does not re-score the general architecture of a
romance — why these two, whether the barrier is structural, whether the
escalation ladder skips rungs, whether the black moment costs anything,
whether both arcs close. Those are `romance`'s dimensions and they apply
to a paranormal romance unchanged. What follows is only what the
paranormal adds on top, and what neither a romance pack nor a fantasy
pack is able to ask.

## Framing

- genre_noun — "paranormal romance novel"

- seed_persona — a paranormal romance novelist who has written vampires, shifters, fae, ghosts and worse across the full heat range, and who never proposes a premise whose barrier would survive being rewritten with a rich, dangerous human in the monster's place
- reader_persona — a paranormal romance reader who finishes 60+ books a year, knows every court, pack hierarchy and bond by name and loves them when they are built properly, and puts a book down the moment the mate bond starts doing the couple's arguing for them
- writer_persona — a working paranormal romance author and series editor who reads for beat placement, for whether the supernatural rules are the ones that actually constrain the couple, and for whether the ending was chosen or granted

## Pillar Dimensions

The relationship is the plot. The supernatural is what makes staying
together expensive, dangerous, or impossible, and it is scored ONLY where
it bears on the couple.

Read that bound literally before scoring anything below. Cosmology,
dynastic history, the geography of the supernatural world, the size of the
pantheon, and how much is implied but unstated are NOT scored here and
their absence is NOT a gap. A paranormal romance that leaves its world
under-specified past the couple's reach is in genre. The question is never
"how much world is there" but "how much of this world touches these two,
and how hard." A fully mapped supernatural polity whose rules never
constrain a scene between the leads scores LOWER on this pillar than three
hard rules that do. Lore that no scene between the leads touches counts
against the score, not for it, exactly as decorative history does in a
fantasy — but here the threshold is the couple, not the plot at large.

### Romancing the Beat, as paranormal romance runs it

`beat_system` is `romancing-the-beat`, not Save the Cat. Its four phases,
with approximate percentage marks, are the structure the outline is judged
against, and the notes say what this genre characteristically does at each:

  1. **Setup** (0-10%) — Both leads in their separate lives, each with a
     goal that is not the other person. Then the No Way / Adhesion. In this
     genre the adhesion is usually supernatural in origin — a debt, a
     claim, a hunt, a shared territory, a bond that has just fired, a
     contract neither can void — and it must bind them physically, not
     merely emotionally.
  2. **Falling in Love** (10-50%) — Inkling of Desire, Deepening Desire,
     Maybe This Could Work, Midpoint of Love. The first disclosure of what
     one of them actually is normally lands in this phase, between 20% and
     40%: early enough that the relationship has to be rebuilt on the
     truth, late enough that there was something to rebuild.
  3. **Retreating from Love** (50-75%) — Inkling of Doubt, Deepening Doubt,
     Retreating, Shields Up. Here the supernatural world reasserts its
     claim: the pack, the court, the council, the treaty, the hunger, the
     terms of the curse. The barrier must reassert itself for reasons the
     first half planted, not because a new authority arrives.
  4. **Fighting for Love** (75-100%) — the Break Up / Black Moment, the
     Dark Night of the Soul, the Grand Gesture, and the resolution: HEA or
     HFN. The Grand Gesture in this genre is normally where the price of
     forever is actually paid — a turning, a binding, a rank surrendered,
     a mortality accepted or refused — and it must be paid by a lead.

An outline in which the supernatural world's claim first appears after 75%
has not written Shields Up; it has written a new antagonist. An outline
whose ending is granted by a rule discovered in the last quarter has not
written a Grand Gesture; it has written a loophole.

### Scored dimensions

- supernatural_indispensability [cap 5] — The supernatural must hold the romance up, not be the set it is staged on. Excellent looks like at least one central obstacle, the black moment, and the price of the ending each being impossible to state without the condition. A gap looks like species labels doing decorative work while the actual barrier is money, class, jealousy, or a job. THE SWAP TEST, applied concretely: rewrite the supernatural lead as a mortal human of equivalent wealth, age, danger and social power — a rich older employer, a soldier back from a war, a man with a criminal past — and change nothing else. Walk the outline. If fewer than three chapters would have to be rewritten, the supernatural is decoration; score 6 max. If the black moment survives the swap unchanged, score 6 max regardless of how much would change elsewhere. Second test: if the documents' supernatural content is species labels and lore with no stated rule that constrains a scene between these two leads, score 5 max. The Genre Contract carries the total-failure version of this test; score this dimension on degree and do not double-count the contract breach here.
- supernatural_constraints [cap 6] — The rules of the condition, judged by what they forbid, cost, or make dangerous between these two. Excellent looks like four to six hard rules, each of which forecloses or prices a specific scene the couple has to play, plus a stated cost of maintaining the condition at all (the feeding, the shift, the tithe, the toll) that a lead pays on the page. A gap looks like rules that describe the supernatural world's furniture rather than the couple's options. Test: for every supernatural rule the documents state, name the scene between the two leads it constrains, forecloses, or prices. If fewer than half of the stated rules can be tied to such a scene, score 6 max. Second test: a writer must be able to draft the couple's three hardest scenes — the first deliberate touch, the disclosure, and the final decision — without inventing a rule that is not written down. If any of the three requires an invention, score 6 max. Third: if the resolution depends on a rule or property first stated after the 75% mark, score 6 max. Do NOT score history, geography, cosmology, or implied depth in this dimension; their absence is in genre and is not a gap.
- bond_versus_choice [cap 6] — Where a fated mate bond, imprint, claiming, prophecy, blood-tie, or any other destined pull exists, THE BOND MAY CREATE THE PULL; IT MUST NOT MAKE THE CHOICE. A destined bond is an anti-barrier: it hands the book its pairing on page one, so the obstacle has to be manufactured elsewhere and the bond has to make the situation worse rather than easier. Excellent looks like a bond that removes the leads' deniability without touching their objections — each lead's reason for refusing survives the bond intact, is stated aloud, and is answered later by a decision, a cost paid, or a belief revised. A gap looks like a lead who objects in chapter 3 and stops objecting because the bond insists. Test: name the objection each lead holds at the 50% mark, then name the scene where it is ANSWERED. If either is instead overridden — "the bond would not let her," "he could not stay away," the pull simply winning — score 6 max. Second test: delete the bond from the outline. If these two would never have got together without it, the bond is the plot rather than the pressure; score 6 max. If the book uses no destined tie at all, which is a legitimate and common choice, score the same axis against whatever is involuntary here — the hunger, the curse's terms, a debt, a haunting, a scent — and ask the same question: are the leads' choices still theirs?
- power_asymmetry [cap 6] — Paranormal romance almost always pairs across a power line: immortal and mortal, predator and prey, pack rank, court rank, made and born, living and dead. Excellent looks like the specific capability the stronger lead holds being named as a mechanism (compulsion, glamour, thrall, a command in the blood, memory alteration, feeding, a claim other supernaturals must honour), the weaker lead knowing it exists and saying so, and the book pricing it in at least two scenes: one where using it would solve a real problem and it is declined or costs something, and one where the weaker lead holds leverage the stronger genuinely cannot take. A gap looks like an asymmetry that is stated and never tested. Test: name the capability, name the chapter where it could have ended a conflict, and name what stopped it. If the answer to the third is "he would never," score 6 max — a safeguard that lives only in the stronger lead's character is not a structure. Second test: if the weaker lead never holds leverage the stronger cannot override, score 6 max. The consent floor is a Genre Contract matter, not a graded one; do not double-count it here.
- revelation_structure [cap 6] — Who knows what the other is, and when, and what knowing costs. Excellent looks like each disclosure being a decision made by the one disclosing, at a price, placed where the escalation ladder can respond, with the documents naming what materially changes in the two chapters after it: who is now in danger, what can no longer be said, who else must now be told or lied to. A gap looks like an accidental reveal that costs nothing — walked in on mid-shift, saw the fangs — or a lead who absorbs the truth within a page. Test: locate in the outline the chapter where each lead first learns what the other is, and the chapter where a third party learns. If any reveal is followed by three or more chapters in which the relationship's state is unchanged, score 6 max. Second test: if the documents cannot name what knowing costs the one who learns — danger, exile, a lie they now maintain, a person they can no longer see — score 6 max. If the black moment IS the reveal and is resolved by the fact simply being accepted, score 6 max. Where both leads know everything from chapter 1 — legitimate when both are supernatural — score instead the first scene in which each sees the other's nature operate at full cost and cannot un-see it, and apply the same tests to those scenes.
- transformation_price [cap 6] — The ending crosses a line this couple cannot cross for free: turning or refusing to turn, mortality accepted, a binding that cannot be undone, a rank or territory surrendered, a claim that closes every other door. Excellent looks like the price stated in the documents before the final quarter, known to both leads, and paid on the page by one of them, measured in something the reader watched them value. A gap looks like forever asserted rather than bought — "they would find a way" — or a cure, exception, prophecy clause, or newly discovered property of the bond that dissolves the cost. Test: name what the happy ending costs, name who pays it, and name the chapter where the reader was first told the price. If that chapter falls after the 75% mark, score 6 max. If the price is paid by a third party or absorbed by the world rather than by a lead, score 6 max. A book that leaves its couple mortal, finite, and out of time can score 9-10 here: the requirement is that the choice was made and priced, never that anyone was turned.

## At Intermediate Length

Four dimensions, and no compressed section: paranormal romance needs a
condition established, a bond tested, and a reveal paid, and five thousand
words cannot hold all three without one of them becoming an assertion. At
novella length it can, which is where the interconnected-standalone
convention actually lives.

The two that go are the two that need a book to demonstrate. A rule set
scored across scenes and a power line traced through structural safeguards
are both novel instruments; what survives is whether the supernatural
matters, whether the leads still choose, what the reveal does, and what
the ending costs.

- supernatural_indispensability [cap 6] — Swap the supernatural condition for an ordinary one — an illness, a criminal record, a job that moves them every year — and re-read the black moment. If it survives the swap unchanged, score 6 max. At this length there is no room for lore that is not doing work in the relationship, so species labels with no stated rule that constrains a scene between these two are the same finding, not a milder one.
- bond_versus_choice [cap 6] — A destined pull may exist; it may not do the choosing. Name the moment each lead chooses the other against something the pull did not decide for them. If either is instead overridden — the bond would not let her, he could not stay away — score 6 max.
- revelation_structure [cap 6] — Who learns what, when, and what it costs them to know. At twelve chapters a reveal followed by two chapters of unchanged relationship state is the whole middle. If any reveal leaves the relationship where it found it for two or more consecutive chapters, score 6 max. If the black moment IS the reveal and resolves by the fact being accepted, score 6 max.
- transformation_price [cap 6] — The ending crosses a line this couple cannot cross for free, and a lead pays. If the price is paid by a third party or absorbed by the world, score 6 max. If it falls after the 75% mark with no earlier scene establishing what it would cost, score 6 max.
- supernatural_constraints — not scored at this band.
- power_asymmetry — not scored at this band.

## Genre Contract

These bind the central couple and the supernatural condition they live
inside. This pack is primary-only, so each promise is written for a book
in which the romance is the main plot.

- The central relationship resolves HEA (happily ever after) or HFN (happy for now): both leads alive or undead by this book's own rules, together, and choosing each other on the page at the end. This genre has a specific trap the promise must cover — an ending in which a lead persists as something that no longer remembers, no longer chooses, or is no longer recognizably that person (a mind wiped, a will bound, a soul rehoused, a resurrection that returns someone else) is a separation ending wearing a reunion's clothes, and breaches this.
- The resolution comes from a decision one or both leads make, not from an external event, a rule change, or a third party removing the barrier for them.
- The bond may create the pull; it must not make the choice. Where the book uses a fated mate bond, imprint, claiming, prophecy, or any other destined tie, every objection either lead holds against the relationship must be answered by a decision, a cost paid, or a belief revised. A book in which an objection stops mattering because the bond overrode it has breached this promise, however satisfying the pull was to read.
- The supernatural element holds the romance up. At least one of the couple's central obstacles cannot be restated without it. A book whose supernatural content could be deleted with the couple's obstacle and the ending left intact has breached: that is a contemporary romance in costume.
- No capability that removes a lead's ability to refuse — compulsion, glamour, thrall, a bond that overrides will, memory alteration, a heat or rut that suspends judgement — is used to obtain intimacy or consent, by either lead, sympathetically or otherwise. The genre may depict such capabilities and should; a book that writes their use on a lead as courtship has breached.
- The rules the ending relies on are established before the final quarter. No cure, exception, loophole, prophecy clause, or newly discovered property of the bond appears in the last 25% to pay a price the book set up.
- The couple this book opens with resolves inside this book. Paranormal romance is written overwhelmingly as interconnected standalones — one couple per volume in a shared world, with the next book's leads planted in this one — and threads left running for a later volume are expected and welcome. This couple's HEA or HFN may not be conditional on one.

## World Sections

Keep this document short and every line working. Every section below must
produce something that CONSTRAINS a choice somewhere in the outline. A
supernatural detail that constrains nothing is decoration and counts
against the score, not for it — and in this genre a long world bible is
usually a sign that the relationship is underbuilt.

- The Supernatural Condition
- What It Costs Between Them
- The Enforcing Body
- Secrecy, Proximity & the Human World
- Private Space & the Body
- The Price of Forever
- Internal Consistency Rules

### The Supernatural Condition
What each lead is, stated as mechanism rather than species. "Vampire" is
not a rule; "cannot cross a private threshold unless someone who sleeps
there invites him, and the invitation can be revoked" is. Write four to
six hard rules, and beside each one name the scene between the two leads
it constrains. A rule that constrains no such scene does not belong in
this document. Then state what the condition costs to maintain — the
feeding, the shift, the tithe, the hours it takes — how often it must be
paid, and what visibly happens when it is not.

### What It Costs Between Them
The most important section in this document. What the condition forbids,
prices, or makes dangerous specifically between these two: touch, sleep,
honesty, argument, distance, daylight hours, hunger near a person you love,
children, the possibility of aging together. Name at least three things
this couple cannot do that an ordinary couple can, and one thing they can
do that no ordinary couple can, and state what each of those four costs
and who pays. If this section could be written about any couple in the
world with the words changed, the premise is a costume.

### The Enforcing Body
Who polices the condition: the pack, the court, the council, the church,
the coven, the bloodline, the treaty. What it can actually DO to these two
— exile, execution, a mark, a debt called in, a child taken, a rank
revoked, protection withdrawn. Its reach must be concrete and near; an
authority that can only disapprove is not an enforcing body. State what it
wants, why its position is defensible from the inside, and what acting
against this couple would cost it.

### Secrecy, Proximity & the Human World
What the human world knows and what it does with what it knows. The terms
of the masquerade, if there is one, and the penalty for breaking it. Then
the adhesion, stated in this genre's terms: the debt, bond, hunt,
contract, shared territory, or job that keeps these two in each other's
path, and what would have to break for them to lose contact entirely.
Name who would notice an absence, on both sides of the line.

### Private Space & the Body
Where these two can be unobserved by both worlds, and where they cannot.
This genre runs on the body: hunger, scent, temperature, heartbeat, the
shift, a wound that closes wrong. Give the condition a sensory
signature — what the supernatural lead's presence does to a room, and what
it does to the other lead's body — specific enough that a scene written in
it could not be relocated to a different pairing without loss. This is the
register in which intimacy is written at whatever heat level the book
chooses, from closed-door to explicit; the pack constrains none of them.

### The Price of Forever
What it would take for these two to have a future, stated as a mechanism
and a bill, before the book begins. Turning, binding, mortality accepted,
abdication, a name given up, a territory surrendered. Who can pay it, what
is lost when they do, who else it costs, and whether it can be undone. If
this section says they will find a way, the book does not have an ending
yet.

### Internal Consistency Rules
Hard constraints a writer must not violate. What breaks a book in this
genre is not a hole in the cosmology; it is the exception invented in
chapter 30, the rule that applies to everyone except the love interest,
the danger that quietly stops being dangerous once the leads are together,
and the bond that means whatever the current scene needs. Write down which
rules have no exceptions, what the supernatural CANNOT do (distance limits
on the bond, daylight, travel time, who can be healed and who cannot, what
the dead cannot say), and the convenient outs this book will not use.

## Cast Requirements

1. **Lead A** — derived from the seed; by default the lead standing lower
   on the power line, human or newly arrived in this world.
   - Full wound/want/need/lie chain
   - Three sliders with justification
   - Arc type (positive/negative/flat)
   - Detailed speech pattern (8 dimensions, with example lines)
   - Physical habits and tells tied to their work, class, and wound
   - At least 2 secrets
   - A goal that exists independently of the other lead
   - What they know about the supernatural world at chapter 1, and what
     they specifically stand to lose by learning more

2. **Lead B** — the same depth, with no shortcuts, plus the condition made
   personal. This genre's characteristic failure is the immortal who is an
   aesthetic: four hundred years old with no century in particular, no
   accent, no debts, no dead. Name the era they were made in and three
   things about the present they still get wrong; what they must do to
   stay what they are, and how long they have been doing it; and one thing
   about their nature they have never told anyone. They need their own
   wound/want/need/lie chain, their own independent goal, and their own
   arc that closes.

3. **The enforcing authority, personified** — one person who speaks for
   the body that polices the condition. Not a villain: their interests are
   legitimate and their position defensible from the inside. Full
   wound/want/need/lie chain, plus what they can do to this couple and
   what doing it would cost them.

4. **One person on the human side with standing** — the confidant, parent,
   sibling, colleague, or child who would be endangered, or lied to
   permanently, by the truth. State what happens to them if the secret
   travels, because that consequence is half the barrier.

5. **A member of the supernatural side who wants what one of the leads
   has** — a rank, a territory, a claim, a bloodline, a mate. Their want
   must be satisfiable by something other than being defeated.

6. **The next book's couple, if this is written as an interconnected
   standalone** — at least one character carrying a want this book will
   not satisfy. They must serve THIS book's plot and be present for the
   couple's sake; a character who exists only to lead the sequel is a
   trailer, not a cast member.

## Drafting Rules

25. The supernatural is felt in the body before it is explained on the page. Every time the condition operates, give it the exact sensory signature established in world.md — the same cold, the same wrongness in the heartbeat, the same smell before a shift — never a generic shiver and never a new one.
26. Never explain a rule the couple is not currently being hurt by. Lore arrives at the moment it costs someone something in the scene. A paragraph of history nobody in the room is paying for is a paragraph to cut, however interesting it is.
27. When a bond, hunger, or compulsion pushes a lead, keep their judgement visible in the prose: what they want, what the pull wants, and which of the two they acted on. A lead who is simply carried is not a character in that scene, and the reader stops being able to credit anything they choose later.
28. This book belongs to one couple, in a world that will hold more. Whoever could carry the next book must want something in THIS one and must matter to THIS plot; and the couple the reader came for must end this book with nothing outstanding.
29. Banned phrases, on top of the base list: "his eyes flashed <colour>", "a low growl rumbled in his chest", "*Mine*, his wolf/the beast snarled", "the bond hummed/thrummed/sang between them", "her scent drove him wild", "he moved with inhuman speed", "she was his mate and that was the end of it", "centuries of loneliness", "he had never wanted anyone like this in five hundred years", "she was the only one who had ever made him feel human". Do not write the beast, the wolf, or the demon as a second speaker who makes the decision; the character makes it.

## Seed Prompt

Persona (adopt while generating):

You are a paranormal romance novelist who has written vampires,
shifters, fae, ghosts, revenants and worse, across contemporary and
historical settings and across the full heat range from closed-door to
explicit. You generate novel concepts that are SPECIFIC, SURPRISING,
and STRUCTURALLY SOUND. Every concept names a supernatural condition
that is a rule with a price, not a species label, and a barrier that
would collapse if the monster were replaced by a rich, dangerous
human — because that replacement is the test the book has to survive.
You never propose a destined bond that also dissolves the obstacle.

Required concept fields (these paranormal romance fields and phrasings
replace the neutral scaffold's versions of the same fields):

WORLD: The pressure system this couple lives inside — the territory,
  the court or pack, the human city it hides in, the work and the
  money. Not a mythology lecture: the specific arrangement that forces
  these two into contact and then makes staying together dangerous.
  Make it SENSORY. Leave the cosmology alone.
CONDITION/COST: What each lead is, stated as a rule and a price rather
  than a species — what the condition forbids, what maintaining it
  costs, who enforces it. Then the one line that matters: what it
  forbids between THESE TWO. Also state who knows what the other is at
  the start, and what learning it will cost. If the same barrier would
  work with a rich human in the monster's place, throw the concept
  away.
COUPLE/BARRIER: Who the two leads are, what specifically draws each to
  the other (something no third party in the book would notice — not
  looks, not proximity), and the STRUCTURAL barrier between them. Name
  the power line they pair across and the exact capability the
  stronger one holds — compulsion, thrall, a claim, a command in the
  blood — and what the weaker one has that cannot simply be taken.
BOND/CHOICE: The involuntary pull, if the concept uses one — mate
  bond, imprint, curse's terms, hunger, a debt, a haunting. State the
  objection EACH lead still holds after the pull exists, and the
  decision that will have to answer it. The pull may make the
  situation worse; it may not make the choice. A concept whose bond
  removes the obstacle is not a story.
PRICE OF FOREVER: What a future between these two costs, as a
  mechanism and a bill — turning, binding, mortality, a rank given up,
  a territory surrendered — who can pay it, and what is lost when they
  do. "They would find a way" is not an answer.
ARCS: For EACH lead, the belief they hold at the start that they
  cannot hold at the end, and why the other person specifically makes
  it untenable. Two arcs, both closing. Neither lead may arrive
  already finished, and immortality is not a substitute for an arc.
TENSION: What's the central conflict? It must be both PERSONAL (what
  each lead stands to lose in themselves) and LARGER (it implicates a
  pack, a court, a family, a city that must not find out). These two
  must be in tension with each other.
THEME: What question does this story explore? Not a message — a
  genuine question with no easy answer.
WHY IT'S NOT GENERIC: One sentence on what makes this different from
  the standard paranormal romance premise.

Aim for DIVERSITY across the ten concepts:
  - Vary the creature, and do not spend more than two concepts on
    vampires or more than two on wolves; ghosts, revenants, selkies,
    psychopomps, saints, and things with no name in English are all
    available
  - Draw on more than one culture's supernatural tradition, and at
    least two from outside Western Europe
  - Vary which side of the power line the reader is anchored to; at
    least one where the supernatural lead is the more vulnerable of
    the two
  - At least one where BOTH leads are supernatural and neither has
    anything to reveal, and at least one where neither is until the
    book makes one of them
  - At least one with no destined bond at all, and at least one where
